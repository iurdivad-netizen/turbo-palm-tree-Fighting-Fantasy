THE CURSE OF HOLLOW'S END · 395 sections · 1,000 simulated games for each of three player types (3,000 total) · seed 20240601

As shipped, the book does not work as a gamebook. A player who just follows the menus wins 1.4% of the time, and a careful explorer only 1.9%. 62% and 78% of their games end in an instant-death section. One section, 245, accounts for 526 of 623 (random) and 671 of 777 (explorer) of those deaths.

The opposite failure shows up too: a bot that knows the book wins 87.5% of its games, in a median of 15 moves (fastest 10), out of a book with 395 sections. There is almost nothing in between to stop it.

Both results have the same root cause: about 32 sections that should be decided by a die or a Luck test are still plain choices (details in Finding 1). Until they are converted, these numbers describe a broken build, not the book's real difficulty, so do not tune balance from this report; fix Findings 1–3 and re-run (Findings 4–6 are smaller, separate data defects).

8 · Findings

1 32 chance gates are still plain choices high

The book says "Roll one die" or "Test your Luck", but the build shows the outcomes as buttons. The player can pick the safe one every time, and the random walker picks the deadly one half the time. 22 sections are already real rolls (several I converted earlier); this census found the rest, because that earlier scan only looked at sections carrying stat-effect markers.

Dice rolls / encounter tables (9): 150, 155, 189, 221, 245, 275, 301, 335, 355
Luck-test traps and obstacles (23): 6, 20, 22, 44, 79, 89, 127, 138, 142, 146, 147, 172, 187, 199, 202, 212, 224, 228, 240, 256, 270, 295, 304
Sections 155, 189, 221, 301, 335 and 355 are six-way random encounter tables: the die should decide whether you meet a Shadow, a hidden cache or a fountain. Today the player simply picks.

Evidence: 623 of the random walker's instant deaths and 777 of the explorer's came through "Turn to 4/39/65"; sections 245, 150, 275 and 189 are dice rolls that offer the death page as a button.

2 Section 245 is a one-in-three death trap with 20 entrances high

245 says "The floor collapses! Roll one die: 1–2 turn to 4 (instant death)". It is linked from 20 sections (14, 16, 27, 71, 81, 101, 112, 135, 149, 166…). It was entered in 673 of 1,000 random games and 746 of 1,000 explorer games. Even after finding 1 is fixed, every pass through 245 will kill a third of players. Either cut the number of links to it, make the roll survivable (damage instead of death), or give the player a way to notice the trap.

3 Arriving at the final door unprepared is a trap high

The final door (395) is offered from the central hubs, so players reach it whenever they like. 98% of the random walker's arrivals and 92% of the explorer's lacked the Silver Key or her true name. Sections 29 and 91 make it worse: their only alternatives are "go to 395 (needs the key)" and "go to 362", so a player without the key is forced into 362, which is the book's biggest killer: 500 deaths in 1,426 fights (65% won; only the Widow's final fight, at 58%, has a lower win rate).

4 The "incomplete victory" ending does not exist medium

Section 362 ends in the text with "THE END (Partial Victory)", but in the data its fight leads on to section 363, an ordinary Phantom Servant ambush, and then back into the hub at 335. Players who survive the three Shadows and the weakened Widow get no ending. Add a proper ending section (or make 362 an ending after the fight).

5 Wrong "ending" flags on 9 mid-game sections medium

Sections 14, 24, 29, 36, 38, 41, 56, 152, 297 are flagged as endings although they carry choices, dice rolls or combat. The reader prints a "Victory!" or "The End" banner on them while the story continues (24 and 56 are the Mayor refusing to help, flagged as victory). This came from the converter reading "(VICTORY!)" or "THE END" in prose. Remove isEnding from these sections.

6 17 sections can never be reached in play medium

Section 180 lists 19 places to explore after the fight, but its victory link jumps straight to 259, so the menu is never shown. Sections 260, 261, 262, 263, 264, 265, 268, 269, 270, 271, 273, 274, 276, 277, 278, 279, 280 have no other entrance. These are the 17 sections the bots never entered in 3,000 games. Fix: remove onVictorySection from the fight so its menu appears after the win (and keep the jump target as one of the menu items).

7 The book has almost no real gating design note

The informed bot reaches the final door in a median of 15 moves. Sections like 45, 301, 378 and 234 are menus with 19–35 options each, so the shortest route is short. That is a property of how the parser flattened the source's "you may also explore…" lists into choices; the original probably relied on the player's own sense of what is worth doing. Worth a design decision rather than a code fix.

9 · Method and limits

  • Engine: the bots drive the real index.html game logic (GameState, fightRound, handleChoice, Luck tests, dice rolls, item use, conditions) in headless Chromium, with delays and DOM rendering switched off.
  • Randomness: a seeded generator (seed 20240601) feeds every die and every bot decision, so the run is reproducible. 1,000 games per policy, 3,000 in total, in 2s.
  • Shared bot habits: flee when STAMINA ≤ 5 and the fight allows it; eat or drink healing items when STAMINA falls below 45% of the maximum (and below 60% before a fight); a game ends after 3,000 moves.
  • Limits: bots are not people. Random under-plays (never plans) and the informed bot over-plays (has read the book), so treat them as bounds. Gold is never spent (the book has no shop). Reading text, noticing hints and risk-aversion are not modelled.
  • Not a balance study: because of Finding 1, the player has choices in the simulation that the book does not intend. Re-run after fixing it.
