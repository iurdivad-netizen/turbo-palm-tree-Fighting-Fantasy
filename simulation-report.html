1 LUCK is a draining resource that the saves depend on high

The book asks for 41 Luck tests plus 8 more nested inside dice rolls, and each costs 1 LUCK. Only 25 sections give LUCK back. Simulated players reach the pit saves with an average LUCK of 7.8. At LUCK 7 a Luck test passes about 58% of the time, at 6 about 42%, at 3 about 8%. The charms help only against the undead and at the binding ritual, so they do not ease the traps.

2 The Widow is still the final wall high

The informed player wins the final fight 47% of the time (87 of 185); 4 of those fights were fought without a magical weapon, which cannot win. She is immune to non-magical weapons and her Wail costs 2 SKILL for the whole fight on an unlucky roll. Letting silver weapons count against her would be the cheapest lever.

3 Many players choose the incomplete ending medium

376 of 3,000 games (13%) end in “Incomplete victory”: the random walker 28%, the careful explorer 9%, the informed player 0%. Section 395 offers to face the Widow without the key and the name, and a player who does is always sent there. If that ending should be rarer, or a failure rather than a finish, change what 395 offers; it is a design choice, not a defect.

4 Menus you come back to punish players who try everything high

Since every side room leads back to its menu, a player can visit every ambush in a menu; before, a room dropped you somewhere else and most of them were skipped by chance. The careful explorer (who visits every new section) won 17.2% → 3.4% and died in combat 39% → 62% of the time. The informed player, who goes straight for the key, the name and a weapon, is unchanged within noise. A human can read the labels (“(fight)”, “−3 STAMINA”) and skip the ambushes, which the bot cannot, so this overstates the difficulty for people. Of the 294 side rooms, 72 are fights; the rest are finds, trap rolls, stat drains and scenery, so exploring everything still costs a lot of STAMINA.

5 Not simulated note

The bots never choose a weapon by hand, read books, drink the Potion of Invisibility, use maps or sneak by choice; the Old Key and the charms count when they are carried. All 22 trap and hazard sections are marked on some map, but only for a player who carries that map.

10 · Method and limits

  • Engine: the bots drive the real engine.js (the same module the reader uses) on the converted book CURSE_OF_HOLLOWS_END.v2.json: fights, Luck tests, dice rolls, item use, conditions, charms, stealth.
  • Randomness: a seeded generator (seed 20240601) feeds every die and every bot decision, so the run is reproducible. 1,000 games per policy, 3,000 in total, in 6s. Every build uses the same seed. A change of about 2.6 points in a win rate is within run-to-run noise, and a rule change also shifts which dice meet which decisions, so small differences are not meaningful.
  • Shared bot habits: flee when STAMINA ≤ 5 and the fight allows it; eat or drink healing items when STAMINA falls below 45% of the maximum (and below 60% before a fight); a game ends after 3,000 moves.
  • What the bots know: read from CURSE_OF_HOLLOWS_END.sim.json: the informed player goes for the Silver Key, the true name and a magical weapon, then the final door; endings come from each section's own ending.
  • Limits: bots are not people. Random under-plays (never plans) and the informed bot over-plays (has read the book), so treat them as bounds. Gold is never spent (the book has no shop). Reading text, noticing hints and risk-aversion are not modelled.
