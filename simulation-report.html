The chance gates are converted, and the picture is sharper. The informed player's win rate fell from 87.5% to 59.9%, because it can no longer pick the safe branch of every roll. The unguided players still almost never win (random 1.4% → 1.2%, explorer 1.9% → 3.0%).

Two structural problems now explain nearly every loss, and neither is a conversion error:

  • Pit traps on the main routes. Sections 245 and 189 each kill one in three players who reach them. Together they killed 32% of the informed player's games.
  • One-way doors to the final boss. Hub 388 offers 9 "final preparation" rooms, and each leads only to the final door (395). Arrive without the Silver Key and her true name and 395 forces the 362 ambush. It was entered 2,441 times in the 3,000 games and killed the player in 34% of its fights.

A player who knows where the key, the name and a magical weapon are wins about 59 games in 100; a player without that knowledge almost never does, and the traps are the least avoidable part of that. These results describe this build and these bots, so read them as a diagnosis rather than a final balance verdict.

9 · Findings

1 Two pit traps decide most games high

Sections 245 and 189 send the player to an instant-death page on a roll of 1–2. Both sit on the central routes: 245 is linked from 20 sections and 189 from 6. The roll is made once per game on the first visit (a trap that has already been sprung is no longer a trap); even so, they killed 320 of the informed player's 1,000 games and 1,118 across all three players. The roll leaves no way to avoid it, no way to notice it, and no cost short of death. Options: turn the death into damage (the book already does this at 41, 107's middle band and 167's survival), make it a Luck test, or cut the links to it.

2 The final-preparation rooms are one-way doors to the boss high

Section 388 ("review everything you've learned about the curse") offers 9 preparation rooms: 381, 382, 383, 384, 385, 386, 387, 389, 390. Each of them has the final door (395) as its only exit (so do 377 and 380, reached from the staircase hub 355, and the Shadow fight in 25). A player can use one room (a healing fountain, a potion, a final fight) and is then carried to the boss, whether or not they hold the Silver Key and her true name. Without both, 395 has one way on: the 362 ambush (three Shadows, then the weakened Widow, SKILL 10, STAMINA 12). 99% of the random walker's 852 arrivals at 395 and 93% of the explorer's 567 were unprepared, including a player who plays carefully and tries to avoid the door. Sections 29 and 91 add to the funnel: without the key their only exit is 362. In total 362 was entered 2,441 times, with 842 deaths (66% of its fights won). Fix: let each preparation room return to 388, and give 395 a way back for a player who is not ready.

3 The "incomplete victory" ending does not exist medium

Section 362 ends in the text with "THE END (Partial Victory)", but in the data its fight leads on to section 363, an ordinary Phantom Servant ambush, and then back into the hub at 335. Players who survive the three Shadows and the weakened Widow get no ending. Add a proper ending section (or make 362 an ending after the fight).

4 Wrong "ending" flags on 9 mid-game sections medium

Sections 14, 24, 29, 36, 38, 41, 56, 152, 297 are flagged as endings although they carry choices, dice rolls or combat. The reader prints a "Victory!" or "The End" banner on them while the story continues (24 and 56 are the Mayor refusing to help, flagged as victory). This came from the converter reading "(VICTORY!)" or "THE END" in prose. Remove isEnding from these sections.

5 17 sections can never be reached in play medium

Section 180 lists 19 places to explore after the fight, but its victory link jumps straight to 259, so the menu is never shown. Sections 260, 261, 262, 263, 264, 265, 268, 269, 270, 271, 273, 274, 276, 277, 278, 279, 280 have no other entrance. These are the 17 sections the bots never entered in 3,000 games. Fix: remove onVictorySection from the fight so its menu appears after the win (and keep the jump target as one of the menu items).

6 Some Luck-test failures lead somewhere that does not fit medium

The conversion follows the book's own cross-references literally, and some of them do not match the story: failing the test in 138, 224, 256, 295 sends you to 97 (a healing shrine that fully restores STAMINA and gives +1 LUCK); failing the test in 142, 304 sends you to 117 (a journal with the curse timeline); failing the test in 228 sends you to 152 (a study with a journal). In those sections an unlucky roll is a reward. I did not guess the author's intent; they need a human decision (most likely a real trap section, such as the dart trap in 58).

7 Still open note

22: section 22 is a Ghoul paralysis test whose SKILL penalty must carry into the next fight; it needs a carry-over penalty mechanic. The informed bot's median of 23 moves to win is still short: menus of 19–35 options (45, 301, 378, 234) come from how the source's "you may also explore" lists were flattened into choices, which is a design question rather than a defect.

10 · Method and limits

  • Engine: the bots drive the real index.html game logic (GameState, fightRound, handleChoice, Luck tests, dice rolls, item use, conditions) in headless Chromium, with delays and DOM rendering switched off.
  • Randomness: a seeded generator (seed 20240601) feeds every die and every bot decision, so the run is reproducible. 1,000 games per policy, 3,000 in total, in 3s. The before-column uses the same seed on the build before the conversion.
  • Shared bot habits: flee when STAMINA ≤ 5 and the fight allows it; eat or drink healing items when STAMINA falls below 45% of the maximum (and below 60% before a fight); a game ends after 3,000 moves.
  • Roll once: encounter hubs (155, 221, 301, 335, 355, 127) and the pit traps (150, 189, 245, 275) roll only on the first visit per game. Other traps roll again on every visit.
  • Limits: bots are not people. Random under-plays (never plans) and the informed bot over-plays (has read the book), so treat them as bounds. Gold is never spent (the book has no shop). Reading text, noticing hints and risk-aversion are not modelled.
